Beyond Corporate Tax:
The Total Economic Tax Contribution

When a company employs thousands of people, it generates far more tax revenue than its corporate income tax bill alone suggests. This tool quantifies the total tax contribution — combining corporate income tax, employer payroll tax, employee income tax withheld, Medicare levy, and GST from employee spending — across Australia's largest companies. As AI-era companies generate high profits with fewer employees, understanding this gap becomes increasingly important for tax policy.

This analysis uses the PwC Total Tax Contribution (TTC) Framework (2005–present), distinguishing Taxes Borne (paid directly by the company) from Taxes Collected (collected by the company on behalf of government). Corporate income tax figures are sourced from the ATO Corporate Tax Transparency dataset (actual reported values). All other figures are modelled estimates clearly marked as such.

🏢 Corporate Income Tax

Source: ATO Corporate Tax Transparency Report 2023–24.
Entities with total income ≥ $100M. Where ATO data is not yet matched, annual report estimates are used and flagged.

Type: Taxes Borne — actual

🏦 Employer Payroll Tax

State payroll tax on wages above state-specific thresholds. Rates: NSW 5.45%, VIC 4.85%, QLD 4.75%, WA 5.50%, SA 4.95%.

max(0, state_wages − threshold) × rate

Type: Taxes Borne — estimated

💼 Employee Income Tax (5-Quintile Model)

Uses a 5-quintile salary distribution per industry (ABS EEH Survey 2023–24), not just the median. This corrects for Jensen's inequality: using only the median understates income tax by ~19–26% in high-dispersion industries (mining, finance).

E[tax(salary)] = Σ 0.2 × tax(salary_q)

Type: Taxes Collected — estimated

🏥 Medicare Levy

2% of gross salary. Applied across the same quintile distribution as income tax. Low-income threshold applies for the lowest quintile earners.

medicare = salary × 0.02

Type: Taxes Collected — estimated

🛒 GST from Employee Spending

Based on ABS Household Expenditure Survey 2021–22. GST covers ~47% of Australian household consumption nationally, but varies by income band: higher earners spend proportionally more on GST-exempt categories (private health, overseas travel, financial services).

after_tax × consumption_rate × gst_coverage × (10/110)

Type: Taxes Collected — estimated

⚠️ Land Tax (optional)

Rough estimate of residential land tax paid by employee homeowners. Disabled by default — enable via the toggle. Highly variable by location and individual circumstances. Treat as indicative only.

employees × homeownership_rate × median_land_value × rate

Type: Taxes Borne — rough estimate

Disclaimer: Estimates are illustrative. Payroll tax, income tax, GST and land tax figures are modelled from published industry and survey data. They are not actual tax liabilities. Superannuation contributions (11.5% of OTE) are a significant employer cost but are not a tax and are excluded from all totals.

Employment Tax Multiplier

Each bubble is one company. X-axis: Australian employee count (log scale). Y-axis: Total TTC ÷ corporate income tax alone. Bubble size reflects total revenue. Colour indicates industry.

Employment Tax Multiplier = Total Tax Contribution / Corporate Income Tax alone.
A multiplier of 2× means the company generates twice as much total tax as its corporate tax bill suggests. High-employment companies (retail, services) typically have multipliers of 3–9×. Low-employment, high-profit companies (mining giants, REITs, AI-era tech) cluster near 1.1–1.3×.
This is the core finding: as AI drives revenue/employee higher, the effective total tax rate (as a proportion of economic output) falls unless tax policy adapts.

AI-Era Signal: Revenue per Employee vs Corporate Tax Dominance

As revenue per employee rises (moving right), corporate tax becomes a larger fraction of the total tax contribution — meaning less tax is generated through employment activity. Bubble size reflects AU employee count.

X-axis: Total revenue ÷ Australian employees (log scale). A useful proxy for "AI-era intensity" — companies moving toward AI-driven operations will shift right over time.
Y-axis: Corporate income tax as a percentage of total tax contribution (with active toggles).
Interpretation: A company in the top-right corner (high revenue/employee, high corp tax %) generates most of its tax revenue through corporate tax alone — employment generates little additional tax. This is the structural vulnerability in current tax systems as AI scales.
