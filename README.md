# Corporate Tax Research 2026
## Total Economic Tax Contribution — Australia

A research tool and interactive visualisation exploring the **total economic tax contribution** of Australia's largest corporations, not just their corporate income tax.

### The Research Question

When a company generates $1 billion in profit while employing 10,000 people, it creates far more total tax revenue than a company with the same profit but 10 employees — because employment generates payroll tax, employee income tax, Medicare levy, and GST from consumer spending. As AI-era companies generate massive profits with fewer employees, this gap matters for tax policy.

**Key metric: The Employment Tax Multiplier** = Total Tax Contribution / Corporate Income Tax alone.

A retailer with 200,000 employees may have a multiplier of 4–9×. A mining company or REIT with high profits but few employees may have a multiplier of 1.1–1.3×.

### Methodology Framework

This analysis uses the [**PwC Total Tax Contribution (TTC) Framework**](https://www.pwc.com/gx/en/services/tax/publications/total-tax-contribution-framework.html), distinguishing:

**Taxes Borne** (paid directly by the company):
- Corporate income tax — from ATO Corporate Tax Transparency data (actual)
- Employer payroll tax — estimated from state rates and employee counts
- Land tax — rough estimate, disabled by default

**Taxes Collected** (collected on behalf of government):
- Employee income tax (PAYG withholding) — estimated using 5-quintile salary distribution
- Employee Medicare levy — estimated at 2% of gross salary
- GST from employee consumer spending — estimated from ABS Household Expenditure Survey

**Income Tax Estimation — 5-Quintile Model**: Rather than applying tax brackets to just the median salary, the model uses the full 5-quintile distribution per industry (ABS Employee Earnings, Hours and Leave Survey). This corrects for Jensen's inequality: the convexity of progressive tax brackets means `E[tax(salary)] > tax(E[salary])`. For high-dispersion industries, using only the median understates income tax by 19–26%.

**GST Estimation**: Based on ABS HES 2021-22. GST covers ~47% of Australian household consumption nationally but varies by income band — higher earners spend more on GST-exempt categories (private health, financial services, overseas travel).

### Quick Start

**Open the web app** (no setup required):
```
webapp/index.html  →  open in any browser
```
The `dataset_2023_24.js` file is pre-generated and embedded in the page.

**Run the full pipeline** (to update data or download latest ATO data):
```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt

# Download ATO Corporate Tax Transparency data
python pipeline/fetch_ato_data.py

# Build the processed dataset (merges ATO data + enriched CSV)
python pipeline/build_dataset.py

# Then refresh index.html in your browser
```

**Update company data**: Edit `data/enriched/companies_enriched.csv` and re-run `build_dataset.py`.

**Update tax rates**: Edit `config/tax_rates.json` (e.g. when super rate changes from 11.5% to 12%).

### Web App Features

- **Tax component toggles**: Enable/disable each tax type to see its contribution in all charts
- **Stacked bar chart**: Top N companies by total tax contribution, stacked by component
- **Employment multiplier scatter**: Log-scale scatter showing multiplier vs employee count
- **AI-era chart**: Revenue per employee vs corporate tax as % of total — shows structural risk
- **Sortable data table**: All metrics, downloadable as CSV
- **Methodology expandable sections**: Full formula explanations per chart

### Caveats & Limitations

1. **All non-ATO figures are estimates** — modelled from industry benchmarks, not company-specific data
2. **Salary distributions** are from ABS industry medians, not each company's actual payroll
3. **GST estimates** assume employees spend as per ABS national survey — actual spending varies
4. **Land tax** is a very rough estimate and is disabled by default
5. **Superannuation** (11.5% of OTE) is a major employer cost but is not a tax and is excluded
6. **Company reporting periods** may not align exactly (some FY June, some December)
7. **International companies** — only Australian income and Australian employees are included
8. The ATO data covers entities with total income ≥ $100M

### Future Expansion

- **Additional countries**: Add new entries to `config/tax_rates.json` + new enriched CSV + new processed JSON
- **Time series**: ATO data is available from 2021-22, 2022-23, 2023-24 — add a year selector to the UI
- **Better employment data**: As more detailed workforce data becomes available, replace estimates with actuals
- **International comparison**: OECD tax wedge comparisons across countries

### Key Findings (FY 2023-24)

Across the ~50 largest Australian companies analysed:

| Finding | Value |
|---|---|
| Total corporate income tax | ~$40.5B |
| Total tax contribution (core) | ~$69.8B |
| Average employment multiplier | ~1.7× |
| Total Australian employees | ~1.07M |
| Highest multiplier | Australia Post (~8.9×) — high employment, low profit |
| Lowest multiplier | GPT Group (~1.1×) — property REIT, minimal staff |
| Woolworths (200k employees) | Multiplier ~4.3× |
| BHP (17k AU employees, $55B revenue) | Multiplier ~1.2× |

The pattern is clear: **employment is a significant tax multiplier**. Policy frameworks that only tax corporate profit without accounting for employment-generated tax miss a large proportion of total economic tax contribution.
